# Auvel design philosophy

Written for the website and reused in the apps' About screens.

## One job, done from across the room

Auvel exists to pause your Mac and change its volume from the sofa. Every screen is judged by one question: can you do that without looking closely at your phone? The dial is large enough to hit by feel, volume moves in steps you can feel, and nothing else competes for your thumb.

## It should feel like it came with your devices

Auvel uses Apple's own type, symbols, colours and controls. It follows Dark Mode, text size, Reduce Motion and VoiceOver because the system already does that well. The only thing we add is one colour, gold, and one shape, the dial.

## Honest about what it knows

Auvel presses your Mac's media keys and sets its volume. It cannot see what is playing, so it never pretends to. When it can't do something, such as change the volume of a display that has no volume control, it says so in plain words.

## Your network, your devices, nothing else

Auvel talks to your Mac over your local network, encrypted, after a one-time code. There are no accounts, no servers, no analytics and no tracking. The App Store label says "Data Not Collected", and the source code shows it.

## Open by default

Both apps and this website are MIT-licensed. If you would rather build Auvel yourself than buy it, the instructions are here. Buying it from the App Store pays for the time it takes to make and maintain.
